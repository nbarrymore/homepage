I am an Assistant Professor of Business, Government and Society at the McCombs School of Business, University of Texas at Austin, and a Research Fellow at the Wharton School's Impact, Value, and Sustainable Business Initiative.

I study corporate governance and how firms choose their leaders. Managers, employees, shareholders, and regulators all shape who leads a firm, and they often disagree about who it should be. My research suggests that the leaders a firm ends up with depend less on what any one party wants than on who controls the decision, what they know about the candidates, and how widely they search. I study these questions on corporate boards and inside firms, often in the setting of gender and racial diversity, where these stakeholders hold distinct and often conflicting preferences.

I am building open data from corporate filings. Several variables researchers need are disclosed in corporate proxy statements but are missing from commercial datasets. I use large language models to extract them directly from the filings, validate them against hand-coded filings, and release them for free, so that any researcher can use the data and check how they were built.

I also study how firms respond to stakeholder and investor pressure on environmental and social issues, including when firms greenwash. I teach Strategic Corporate Social Responsibility, Strategy Beyond Markets, and Issues and Politics in American Government at The University of Texas. Before my PhD in Managerial Economics at the University of Maryland, I was the founding principal of a charter high school in Los Angeles.

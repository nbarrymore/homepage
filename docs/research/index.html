Research

My research asks three questions:

  • Who decides who sits on a corporate board? How shareholders, activists, and regulators shape board appointments.
  • Why do women and racial minorities advance differently? How the structure of evaluation and promotion, inside firms and across them, shapes who reaches leadership.
  • How can we build reliable, auditable, and open data infrastructure for governance research?

Boards and corporate governance

A general approach for creating structured data from corporate filings

With Nicholas Hallman.

We use large language models to extract structured data from proxy statements at about $0.10 per filing, validated against hand-coded filings and benchmarked against commercial vendors.

Supported by a McCombs Research Excellence Grant.

Short-term quotas, long-term change? Evidence from California's repealed board diversity mandate

With Rachelle C. Sampson. Submitted. SSRN

Using the 2022 court ruling that ended California's board gender mandate, we find evidence that the mandate pushed firms to search more widely for directors, not that it led them to appoint ineffective ones.

Ratified change: How the revealed preferences of shareholders shape activist campaigns

With Mark DesJardine and Mae McDonnell. Earlier version in AOM Proceedings (2025).

An activist seeking board seats needs the votes of other large shareholders. After the Big Three asset managers committed to board gender diversity in 2017, the drop in women's share of board seats that activist campaigns had produced fell by more than two-thirds and was no longer distinguishable from zero.

Advancement inside the firm

Gender and competitiveness when earning for others: Experimental evidence and implications for sponsorship

With Cristian L. Dezső and Ben King. Strategic Management Journal, 2022, 43(5): 905–934. SMJ

When participants compete on behalf of subordinates, the gender gap in competitiveness vanishes.

Diverse routes to leadership: Gender and racial differences in internal and external promotions

With Tiantian Yang. Revise and resubmit.

Using career histories of 29.1 million U.S. workers, we find that women's promotion gap lies in moves to new firms, while racial minorities' gap lies in promotion within their current firm.

Are there gender differences in ambition? Evidence from an online experiment

With Cristian L. Dezső and Yongyi Lin. SSRN

In a pre-registered, incentivized experiment, we find no significant gender differences in ambition, defined as goal setting and goal pursuit.
